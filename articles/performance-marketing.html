Performance Marketing 101: Making Every Rupee of Ad Spend Accountable

Performance marketing isn't a specific channel — it's a discipline. Every campaign, on every platform, gets tracked against one number: what it actually returned.

● 6 min read ● Updated Sep 2026 ● BTech Marketing Wala

"Performance marketing" gets used loosely, but the definition is simple: marketing where results are measurable, and budget moves toward what's actually working — not what feels good or looks impressive in a slide deck.

The funnel behind the strategy

Different channels do different jobs. Search ads catch people who already know what they want. Social ads and video build awareness with people who don't know you yet. Retargeting brings back visitors who almost converted. Treating all of this as one undifferentiated "marketing budget" is where most businesses waste money — a performance marketing approach maps each channel to the stage of the funnel it's actually good at.

The metric that matters: ROAS

Return on ad spend (ROAS) answers one question — for every rupee spent, how much revenue came back? A campaign with a lot of clicks and low ROAS isn't working, no matter how good the engagement numbers look. A campaign with modest reach but strong ROAS is one to pour more budget into.

This only works if tracking is actually set up correctly — conversion tracking on your website, proper attribution across channels, and clean reporting that ties spend to results, not just activity.

Why creative, targeting and landing page all have to work together

A performance marketing strategy fails quietly when any one piece is weak, even if the others are strong:

  • Great targeting with weak creative — people see the ad and scroll past.
  • Great creative sent to the wrong audience — expensive clicks that never convert.
  • Great ads sending traffic to a slow or confusing website — visitors arrive and leave before converting.

This is the core reason we run websites, creative and ads from one team — a performance marketing strategy is only as strong as its weakest link, and that link is rarely obvious until someone is looking at the whole funnel at once.

Frequently asked

Is performance marketing only for e-commerce?

No — it applies anywhere a result can be tracked, including lead generation for service businesses, sign-ups, calls or WhatsApp enquiries.

How often do you report on results?

Weekly, in plain language — what we spent, what it earned, and what we're changing next. No jargon-filled decks.

What if a channel isn't performing?
